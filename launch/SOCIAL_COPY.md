## LinkedIn

I built Signal because my LinkedIn feed was getting better at earning reactions and worse at earning my attention.

Signal is a Chrome extension that scores each visible post from 0 to 100 against the interests and goals you choose. It can collapse, hide, blur, or simply label posts below your threshold.

What mattered to me:

- Every score has an explanation.
- The core scorer runs locally and works offline.
- There is no analytics or advertising.
- Account sync and external AI are optional.
- AI keys stay on the device and are never synced.
- The complete code is open source.

I am looking for early feedback, especially on posts it scores incorrectly and whether the explanations help you tune the feed.

## X thread

1/ LinkedIn decides what is likely to hold your attention. Signal lets you decide what deserves it.

2/ Every visible post gets a 0 to 100 score based on your role, skills, interests, target companies, and goals. Low-value posts can be collapsed, hidden, blurred, or left visible with a score.

3/ The score is not a black box. Open a badge to see the category, positive and negative reasons, and the dimensions behind the result.

4/ The built-in scorer runs locally. AI is optional and uses a provider you choose. Account sync is optional too. AI keys never enter the sync payload.

5/ Signal is open source and has no analytics or ads. I would love feedback on misclassified posts and the controls you want next.

Title: Show HN: Signal, an open-source relevance filter for the LinkedIn feed

Body:

I built a Manifest V3 Chrome extension that scores visible LinkedIn posts against a user-defined professional profile and filters posts below a chosen threshold.

The default path is a local heuristic model, so it works offline and does not need an account. It can optionally use any OpenAI-compatible endpoint for ambiguous posts. It also has explainable score badges, direct feedback, four filtering modes, sponsored-content detection, and a local dashboard.

The source is here: https://github.com/hithere-devs/signal-linkedin

I would appreciate technical feedback on selector resilience, scoring quality, and the privacy boundaries around optional sync and AI.
